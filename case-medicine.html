Reminders, prefilled.

Medicine reminders in a public health records app, set up straight from the prescription. Good research, and one screen I'd now change.

Overview

A public health records app could already read a prescription with AI. Setting a reminder was still a separate journey, one medicine at a time, typed by hand, so people put it off. I redesigned the flow so the reminders come from the prescription itself, and only the missing details are left to fill in.

infoRecreated and kept neutral. The real app isn't named, and every screen here is redrawn as original UI with made up medicines and people. The sketches are my own from the project. I did it part time over about a month, next to my full-time job, on my own with a course mentor.

The Starting Point

It started at home. My grandmother wasn't well, and even with all of us keeping track, a medicine would sometimes slip. My first idea was a smart pill box, with a light and a beep for morning, afternoon and night.

Pill organisers already exist and cost very little. My mentor also pointed out that I was designing for one person I knew. So I went back to asking people.

The Research

Four conversations and two surveys. Each one moved the problem somewhere I didn't expect.

The Problem

A public health records app already stored prescriptions and could read them with AI. Reminders were still a separate journey, one medicine at a time, typed by hand. In my storyboard, the user gives up halfway, and a dose gets missed.

How might we help people set up medicine reminders with as little typing as possible, so they take their medicines on time?

The Design

I mapped the journey on paper first, then built it in Figma. Two parts did most of the work.

01Reminders From the Prescription

Right after a prescription is read, one optional question: set reminders for these medicines? Every medicine arrives filled in from the prescription. Only what it doesn't say is left to add.

02Today's Medicines on Home

Home gets a card for the next dose. Taken, later or skip, each one tap. Every choice can be undone, because a wrong tap on a medicine matters more than most.

What I'd Change

The reminder screen is the part I'd undo. I made it look like an incoming call so nobody could miss it, thinking of older users like my grandmother.

Looking back, it makes people work out whether it's a call or a reminder, which is extra effort right when they need it least. Swiping to mark a dose as taken is also easy to miss. Next time I'd keep every action, snooze, skip, mark as taken and back, but put them on a plain app screen with clear buttons, then test both with older users.

What I madeA reminder that looks like a call

What I'd try nextThe same actions, as clear buttons

What I Learned

Not my best outcome, and the project that taught me the most about how I work.

  • inventory_2Alone is a closed box

    With no one testing it, my first idea for the screen stayed my only idea.

  • exploreLet the research steer

    The surveys pointed at bigger problems, like records and routines. I settled for a narrow fix.

  • airGive it room

    Under work stress I got stuck making screens. Next time, smaller steps and real users after each one.

Slow down, keep asking, and keep the people it's for in the room.
