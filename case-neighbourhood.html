Overview

This was a new app for one neighbourhood: local offers, events and stores in one place, with a reason to open it every day. The first release had no payments, so people browse, save, walk in and redeem in person. I built the design system, shaped the flows with another designer, and finished the design with the developers. It was handed off but never launched.

lockThis is a recreation, not the real app. The project is under NDA, so I redrew every screen as a simplified sketch with fictional stores, people and content, and the client is scribbled out like this . The thinking, flows and decisions are real. Happy to talk through the real work.

The Brief

The brief packed a lot into a first release. The client's reference boards were loud and heavy on colour, and one question kept coming back: how to stop the same offer being claimed again and again.

checklist What was asked
  • Phone sign in and a daily home feed
  • Offer and store pages, events and alerts
  • A personal space for saved and redeemed things
forum The client's main worry
  • How do we stop one offer being claimed again and again? (paraphrased)
  • Every offer was for a physical store, not online.

Constraints

A new product with a short runway, and a lot still undecided.

  • devicesThree surfaces

    An app, a download website and email, all needing to feel like one product.

  • helpPartners still open

    Which stores would join wasn't decided, so nothing could depend on tools they'd run.

  • signal_cellular_connected_no_internet_0_barUsed out and about

    Location off, no network, an old app version. Each needed a designed state.

03The App

Three moments from the app itself, each shown as a short walkthrough: getting in, finding something nearby, and events with the screens for when things go wrong.

Getting In, Fast

Three short intro slides, then a phone number and a one-time code. The alerts opt-in sits on the sign in screen, email is optional, and the profile step can be skipped. Interest picking was designed early but left out of the final flow.

Finding Something Nearby

Home answers "what's good today?" with a featured offer, categories and what's trending. One search covers offers, stores and events, and every store has its own page with its offers, events and videos.

Events, and When Things Go Wrong

Events are interest only, since tickets and payments were out of scope. Offline, update and maintenance screens were designed before handoff, in the same visual language as the rest of the app.

04Redeem Only When You're There

The client needed control, so one offer couldn't be redeemed again and again by anyone, anywhere. A code system run by every store wasn't realistic while partners were undecided, so redeeming is tied to being at the store. It's a first-phase answer, and no failure is a dead end.

05Same System, Website and Email

The download website and the verification email reuse the app's type, colour and buttons, so the first thing someone sees outside the app already feels like it.

Key Decisions

Five calls that shaped the app.

  1. where_to_vote01
    Redeem at the store, not anywhere

    Control over redemptions, without codes from undecided partners.

  2. dark_mode02
    Dark first, colour only for actions

    With photos on every screen, colour had to mean "you can act here".

  3. bolt03
    Let people in fast

    Every screen before the feed is a chance to lose someone.

  4. star04
    Interest, not tickets

    No payments yet, but people still see what's filling up.

  5. alt_route05
    Design the unhappy paths up front

    Developers got a screen for every case, not a guess.

The Outcome

The design was complete and with the developers, but the app didn't go live, so there are no numbers to share. Here's what the project taught me.

  • tuneChange it once

    Feedback on colour and buttons was fixed in the components and reached every screen.

  • handshakeOwn the handoff

    Working with the developers kept decisions consistent after the team got smaller.

  • flagFirst answers are allowed

    Redemption wasn't settled, so we designed the simplest honest check and left room to change it.
