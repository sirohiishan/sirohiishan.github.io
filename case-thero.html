Overview

Thero is a to-do app I first made for my own college work. Every task gets its own priority, so the list always answers one question: what do I do next? I designed it in Figma, built it in SwiftUI with Cursor, and took it through App Store review. It stayed live for about a year.

smartphoneReal screens. This is my own project, so everything here is the shipped app. Only the sample tasks are made up, and the home screen behind reminders and widgets is blurred.

Why I Built It

Cursor was new, and I wanted to see how far one designer could take an idea alone: from Figma, to working code, to the App Store. A to-do app was small enough to finish, and something I'd open every day.

  • drawDesign it

    Flows, a type scale and colour tokens in Figma.

  • codeBuild it

    SwiftUI with Cursor, including widgets and notifications.

  • rocket_launchShip it

    App Store review, a privacy policy and a way to reach me.

The Problem

Most to-do apps ask you to mark each task High, Medium or Low. On a busy day nearly everything is High, so you still have to work out what comes first. Thero gives every task its own number instead, so the list tells you what's next.

Constraints

No team, no deadline, and a lot I hadn't done before.

  • personJust me

    Design, code and release, so every extra feature was mine to build and keep working.

  • schoolNew to Swift

    Cursor wrote the code. My job was to judge it and keep it on the design.

  • policyApp Store rules

    A privacy policy, a support contact, and account deletion for any app with accounts.

The Solution

The app in the order you meet it: getting started, priorities, reminders, widgets and progress. Each part plays as a short walkthrough of the real app.

01Getting Started

No account and no sign up. Three promises up front, a name that never leaves the phone, then a short tutorial that points at each control. It can be replayed from Settings.

02Priority, Not Levels

Every open task has its own priority, and priority 1 comes first. Give a new task priority 3 and everything from 3 down moves one place. A priority can't go past the count of open tasks plus one, so there are never gaps or ties. Finished tasks drop below the open ones.

03Reminders That Act

A reminder can finish the job by itself: mark the task complete, or push it by 15 minutes, an hour or to tomorrow morning, right from the notification. A morning nudge and a 9 PM prompt to plan tomorrow keep the list moving.

04Widgets

The home screen widgets show how many tasks are left today and the next ones in order. Tick a task off from the widget without opening the app. A lock screen widget keeps the count in view.

05Progress

My Month turns done versus total into one score, with a month picker. The calendar marks each day as complete or not, so a bad week is easy to spot.

Figma to Code

Cursor kept drifting from the design until I gave it rules. I turned my Figma style guide into colour and type tokens first, and told it to use only those. The shipped code has about 330 colour token references and only 8 raw colours, and the type scale matches Figma exactly.

  • Black#000000
  • Black 2#1B1B1B
  • Black 3#323232
  • White#FFFFFF
  • White 2#8F8F8F
  • Coral red#FF7979
  • Pastel blue#7998FF
  • tokenTokens first

    Colour and type became code before any screen did.

  • rounded_cornerThe notched card

    SwiftUI had no shape for it, so I split it into four corner SVGs and flat fills, scaled to the screen width.

  • heightFill every iPhone

    The task card stretches to the screen's height, from the smallest iPhone to the largest.

Key Decisions

Five calls that kept a one person app small and finished.

  1. format_list_numbered01
    Priority, not levels

    A short list needs an order, not buckets.

  2. lock02
    Keep data on the phone

    Early builds had Google sign in. I dropped accounts: no sign up wall, and no personal data to look after.

  3. touch_app03
    Finish it from anywhere

    Notifications and widgets can complete a task without opening the app.

  4. token04
    Tokens before code

    Cursor works best inside rules.

  5. content_cut05
    Cut the integrations

    Calendar and Notion sync were in the first design. One app done well came first.

The Logo

Five directions, one winner. The final icon is a tick breaking out of its frame: finish the task, then get on with your day.

  • A T with a tick

    Tied the name to done, but you had to decode it, and it fell apart small.

  • Pixel black and white

    Felt strict and heavy. An app you open all day should feel calm.

  • A notebook

    Familiar, but a notebook is for writing things down. Thero is for getting them done.

  • A centred tick

    Balanced, but it just sat there. It needed a sense of moving on.

The Outcome

Thero passed review and stayed on the App Store for about a year. What I took away from it:

  • ruleRules make AI useful

    Cursor got good once the design system was in the code first.

  • content_cutScope is design

    Dropping accounts and sync made the app better, not smaller.

  • checklistShipping has a checklist

    Privacy policy, support, review notes. None of it is in Figma.

Designing it was half the work. Shipping it taught me the other half.
